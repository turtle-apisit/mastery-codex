# Software Complexity and Intangibility

## Summary

Two underlying traits of software that everything else in the course reacts to.

**Complex** — *consisting of many different and connected parts, not easy to
analyze or understand.* The instruction that follows is precise:

> Complexity must be **handled/managed**, **not eliminated**.

**Intangible** — *unable to be touched; not having physical presence; difficult
or impossible to define or understand; vague and abstract.* The consequence:

> Largely intangible → **difficult to visualize, test, and estimate**.

**Artifacts.** An artifact is *an object made by a human being* — which means
mistakes always happen, along with miscommunication and misunderstanding.
Therefore:

- Easy to create **with poor design**
- Easy to modify but **difficult to do it right**

## Key points

- The complexity claim is a claim about limits: no technique in the course
  removes complexity, they only make it manageable (modularity, abstraction,
  separation of concerns, stepwise refinement).
- The intangibility claim explains three separate later problems at once: why
  estimation is hard, why testing is hard, and why the specification document is
  so heavily emphasised.

## Watch out for

- Line of Code (LoC) is offered as a *visualisation* of complexity, not as a
  measure of quality or productivity.

## Prerequisites

None — this is a root concept for the subject.
