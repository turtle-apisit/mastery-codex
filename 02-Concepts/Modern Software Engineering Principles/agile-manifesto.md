# Agile Manifesto

## Summary

Agile is considered **value-driven, lightweight, flexible, and adaptive**.

**The Agile Manifesto for Software Development:**

> We are uncovering better ways of developing software by doing it and helping
> others do it. Through this work we have come to value:
>
> - **Individuals and interactions** over *processes and tools*
> - **Working software** over *comprehensive documentation*
> - **Customer collaboration** over *contract negotiation*
> - **Responding to change** over *following a plan*
>
> That is, **while there is value in the items on the right, we value the items
> on the left more.**

## Key points

- The closing sentence is the part most often dropped, and it is the part that
  makes the manifesto defensible. Agile does **not** say documentation, plans, or
  contracts are worthless — it says they lose when they conflict with the item on
  the left.
- Each pair reads as a direct rejection of one waterfall property: documentation-
  driven, contract/specification-as-truth, plan-fixed-in-advance.

## Watch out for

- Agile is presented here as a **property of IID**, not a separate lifecycle
  model. In this course it follows from iteration and incrementation rather than
  standing beside them.
