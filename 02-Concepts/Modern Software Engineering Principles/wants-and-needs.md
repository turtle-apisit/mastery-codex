# Wants and Needs

## Summary

*"I want an iPad Pro"* versus *"I need an iPad Pro."*

The **business need is the end result or purpose** — revenues, profits, costs,
sustainability, ranking, recognition. It is **"the why we are doing this."**

Example of a need stated properly:

> Help the marketing team reach out to non-paying customers and offer new
> products to convert them to paying customers.

Notice it names no feature and no screen.

**Implicit vs explicit:**

- **Implicit** — assumptions **not directly expressed**.
- **Explicit** — **stated clearly and in detail**, leaving no room for confusion
  or doubt.

**Needs change.** Pandemic, economy, geopolitical tension — the world moves.
**Change is inevitable.**

## Key points

- The aim of the requirements activity is to determine the client's **needs**,
  **not what the client wants**.
- A need is a **high-level representation** of the requirement needed; individual
  requirements are derived from it.
- Because needs change over time, any process that assumes a frozen specification
  is fighting the nature of the problem — this is the seed of the
  [[moving-target-problem]] and the argument for iterative development.

## Watch out for

- "We want AI" is, in the AI-enabled software class, called **the purest want you
  will ever be handed** — a want with no stated need behind it at all.
