# Software Engineering Code of Ethics

## Summary

Developers and maintainers need to be hard working, intelligent, sensible, up to
date and, **above all, ethical**.

**The eight principles** (ACM/IEEE Software Engineering Code):

1. **PUBLIC** — act consistently with the **public interest**.
2. **CLIENT AND EMPLOYER** — act in the best interests of client and employer,
   **consistent with the public interest**.
3. **PRODUCT** — ensure products and modifications meet the **highest
   professional standards possible**.
4. **JUDGEMENT** — maintain **integrity and independence** in professional
   judgment.
5. **MANAGEMENT** — managers and leaders shall subscribe to and promote an
   ethical approach to management of development and maintenance.
6. **PROFESSION** — advance the **integrity and reputation of the profession**,
   consistent with the public interest.
7. **COLLEAGUES** — be **fair to and supportive of** colleagues.
8. **SELF** — participate in **lifelong learning** and promote an ethical approach
   to practice.

## Key points

- Note the ordering and the qualifier: **PUBLIC comes first**, and CLIENT AND
  EMPLOYER is explicitly **subordinated** to it with the phrase *"consistent with
  the public interest"*.

## Watch out for

**The KMUTT Health Centre case study** — worth being able to reconstruct:

- The overbooking rule **releases a slot to the waiting list** when a booking is
  flagged as a likely no-show, and if the original patient **does** arrive they
  may be **turned away**.
- The model flagged students from one faculty at **nearly three times the average
  rate** — because that faculty's compulsory labs run 09:00–12:00, overlapping
  most of the clinic's slots, so those students had historically missed more
  appointments.
- The developer, N., **raised it once** in the review meeting. The lead said
  *"Good catch — we'll look at that later."* **It was not written down. No action
  was recorded.** She did not raise it again. The system went live.
- Eight months later: *"Health Centre app turns away sick engineering students,
  data shows."*

The exam question is **what should she have done instead** — and the answer is
found in principles 1, 4 and 7, plus the observation from Class 5 that **nothing
in this case required any machine learning to understand**.
