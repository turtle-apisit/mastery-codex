# Requirement Elicitation Techniques

## Summary

Four techniques:

1. **Interview** — the **primary** technique
2. **Questionnaires**
3. **Examination of business forms/documents**
4. **Direct observation**

**Interviews in detail.** The requirements team meets with the client and users
to extract all relevant information.

*Two types of question:*

- **Close-ended** — require a specific answer
- **Open-ended** — posed to encourage the interviewee to speak out

*Two types of interview:*

- **Structured** — specific preplanned questions, frequently close-ended
- **Unstructured** — questions posed in response to the answers received,
  frequently open-ended

> An interview that is **too unstructured will not yield much relevant
> information.**

*Interviewing is not easy:*

- The interviewer must be **fully familiar with the application domain**
- The interviewer must **remain open-minded at all times**
- Afterwards, the interviewer **must prepare a written report** — and it is
  **strongly advisable to give a copy to the person who was interviewed**

**The other three:**

- A **questionnaire** is useful when the opinions of **hundreds** of individuals
  need to be determined
- **Examination of business forms** shows how the client **currently does
  business**
- **Direct observation** of employees performing their duties can be useful

## Key points

- Structured pairs with close-ended, unstructured pairs with open-ended — but
  "frequently", not always.
- Giving the written report back to the interviewee is a **verification step**,
  not a courtesy: it is how you find out you misunderstood them.

## Watch out for

- Technique choice follows the number of people and the kind of information:
  hundreds of opinions → questionnaire; how work is actually done → observation
  or business forms; depth and nuance → interview.
