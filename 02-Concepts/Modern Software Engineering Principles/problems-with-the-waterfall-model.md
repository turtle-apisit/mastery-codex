# Problems with the Waterfall Model

## Summary

**1. Validation is performed at the end.**

- **High risk of not getting the right product**
- You rely on a **specification document** which is either **too technical or too
  informal** — because the software is intangible and complex

**2. The relay-runner problem.**

- Relying on **passing documents** → **misunderstanding**
- **Programmers must wait for BA/SA**

**3. Value arrives only at the end.**

- You must **wait until the end of the project to get the product, and thus the
  value**
- If the project is **terminated midway → no usable product**
- **Changes are unavoidable → wastes**

**The underlying assumptions** — and why they fail:

- You **can specify a system in advance**
- There will be **no changes later**

Both hold only for a **1-shot project**. Fred Brooks is cited here.

## Key points

- Every one of these problems is a direct consequence of the two choices that
  also give waterfall its strengths: **documentation-driven** and **one delivery
  at the end**.
- This list is the entire motivation for
  [[iterative-and-incremental-development]] — read it as the problem statement
  that IID is the answer to.

## Watch out for

- The slide still asks *"What is it suitable for?"* — waterfall is not simply
  wrong. It suits projects where requirements genuinely can be fixed in advance
  and change is genuinely rare.
